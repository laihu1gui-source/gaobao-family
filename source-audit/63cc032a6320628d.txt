Practitioner Assessment
Welcome to the Occupational Therapy Council of Australia Ltd (OTC), who are the independent assessment authority for occupational therapists in Australia and whose aim is to ensure that all occupational therapists can deliver safe and culturally responsive care.
Are you an internationally qualified occupational therapist looking to work in Australia?
This webinar, recorded on 26 August 2026, provides an overview of the pathways for internationally qualified occupational therapists seeking to migrate to Australia and/or obtain registration to practise.
Presented by the Occupational Therapy Council of Australia (OTC) and the Occupational Therapy Board of Australia (OTBA), the webinar covers:
Assessment for Migration
qualification assessment and registration pathways
Assessment of Competence
the roles of the OTC, OTBA and Ahpra
key information to help internationally qualified occupational therapists understand the pathway to working in Australia.
The OTC conducts two types of assessments for occupational therapists, including internationally qualified occupational therapists and occupational therapists who completed their qualification in Australia as international students.
Assessment for migration (Skills assessment)
A desktop assessment required by the Department of Home Affairs for skilled migration visas where a skills assessment is a visa requirement. This applies regardless of where the qualification was completed, including Australian qualifications, and may still be required even where an applicant holds general registration in Australia.
Assessment of competence
A period of supervised practice for internationally qualified occupational therapists, either referred by the Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency (Ahpra) under the Qualification Assessment Pathway, or managed by the OTC for eligible legacy applicants who completed Stage 1 prior to 10 October 2025 and hold a valid skills assessment. This assessment forms part of the requirements for eligibility for general registration in Australia.
These assessments have different purposes, requirements and outcomes. Applicants should ensure they select the assessment(s) relevant to their circumstances.
The assessment of international qualifications for registration purposes is undertaken by the Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency (Ahpra).  Please refer to the
Ahpra website
for more information.
